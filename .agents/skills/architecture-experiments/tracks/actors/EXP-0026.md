## Decision Question

How much do the selected 12-Step DEOS reference ceiling and lockstep `24/48` Opening bounds reduce EXP-0025 nonterminal middle-Step proof and maximum lifecycle geometry, and does that changed baseline clear the W1-derived `6,361` comparison allowance or expose a narrower next physical owner?

## Governing Specification

- `Exact section`: `template/pallets/actors/docs/specification.en.md` → `13.3 Runtime configuration`; `docs/actors-performance-assurance.specification.en.md` → `4 Contract Complexity Matrix`.
- `Invariant`: Preserve Q1, one class-neutral FIFO, exact Step semantics, atomic independent committed prefixes, current Contract/Run/state-hold ownership, host configurability up to 255 Steps, and complete dispatchability of every DEOS genesis System Actor Contract.
- `Reopen trigger`: Any candidate requiring changed Step semantics, Task behavior, FIFO order, causal timing, duplicated Contract authority, or a non-generic pallet ceiling must reopen Specification rather than remain a physical consequence measurement.

## Context and Hypothesis

- `Current architecture`: EXP-0025 C1 uses ticket-addressed C32 control frames and C6 four-Step tail chunks. Its P32 middle Transfer is semantically exact at `1,127.1m / 6,876.52` per Actor for 100, while Transfer P0 is `902.4m / 6,187.96`.
- `Reason`: DEOS selected 12 Steps as sufficient local linear-program expressiveness, with larger compositions represented by Actor graphs. The previous `32/64/128` maximum profile is now `12/24/48`; EXP-0004 and EXP-0021 selection claims are invalidated or superseded, but their measurements remain historical baselines.
- `Hypothesis`: Smaller maximum-proven Contract/run values reduce P12 middle-Step estimated ProofSize by at least 5%, potentially below `6,361`, while one-Step P0 remains non-regressing and maximum create/close/reconstruct geometry falls materially.
- `Materiality`: Report every dimension independently. A `>=5%` middle complete-path ProofSize reduction or removal of one maximum-bound proof owner is material; clearing `6,361` is useful comparative headroom, not a universal middle-Step acceptance promise.

## Statistical / Variance Notes

Generated Weight evidence is deterministic model output under the declared 50×20 method. Corrected P12 middle uses coefficient standard error `549,709`, measured proof `2,281 + 1,596n`, and estimated proof `8,088 + 6,553n`. Grouped RefTime applies production RocksDB Weight to four calls (`3×32 + 4`), 1,028 reads, and 624 writes. Corrected lifecycle ranges are exactly `c=0..3`; no clamped observations enter their linear models. Runtime test duration is not decision evidence.

## Result

Corrected P12 leaves middle estimated and measured ProofSize exactly unchanged at `217,784 / 53,353` for n=32 and `6,876.52 / 1,687.24` per Actor when grouped to 100. It shrinks the `ActorRunPayload` maximum from 2,533 to 1,491 bytes, but `ActorContractTailChunk` remains `4,070 / 6,545`; grouped RefTime improves only `0.7845%`. P0 keeps `6,187.96` proof and improves RefTime `1.0336%`. Maximum P12 create/close/reconstruct, however, reduce estimated proof about `51%` and RefTime `45.9–48.2%` against P32 because complete tail count falls from eight to three. `ActorStateHolds` remains a separate 152-byte maximum owner. W4 passes through the runtime suite and explicit maximum-tail genesis witness. The corrected benchmark Wasm is `d555298a`, and every retained artifact uses its reachable `c=0..3` source.

## Interpretation

P12 is a material maximum-lifecycle simplification and a throughput-path null result. It removes five maximum tail chunks from lifecycle operations and halves their proof, while preserving P0 and middle semantics without component-wise regression. It does not change the middle current-plus-successor `ActorContractTailChunk` owner, does not lower `6,876.52`, and does not clear `6,361`. The independently selected P12 product policy is physically cheaper at its maximum, but the next throughput experiment must change exactly one retained tail/state-hold mechanism rather than claim that the ceiling alone solved W1.

## Decision

Reject the P12-only middle optimization hypothesis: W1 remains `6,876.52` and does not clear `6,361`. Retain P12 as the independently selected DEOS product policy and as the measured baseline because it preserves W2/W4 while materially reducing maximum lifecycle cost. Open EXP-0027 with only immutable contract-body state-hold sourcing changed.

## Validity

- `Establishes`: Exact P12 W1/W2 null-result geometry, material maximum lifecycle savings, W4 dispatchability, and the retained tail/state-hold owner.
- `Does not establish`: EXP-0025 acceptance, integrated W1 throughput, universal middle-Step capacity, or benefit in another host runtime.
- `Known limitations`: P32 and P12 use different runtime-config identities by design. Invalid clamped W3 artifacts are retained only as diagnosis and excluded. Production metadata/client regeneration follows the accepted runtime handoff.
- `Invalidation triggers`: Contract/run/storage layout, C32/C6 geometry, P12 bounds, Task/effect profile, toolchain, benchmark method, block Weight, or proof model changes.
- `Residual qualitative insight`: Product-level limits can release maximum proof/state headroom without forcing a generic pallet ceiling, but actual short-path benefit must be measured.

## Follow-Up / Next Gradient

```text
P32 middle 6,876.52 → selected P12 bound profile → maximum-proven value reduction
→ rerun exact middle/P0/lifecycle owners → name retained binding proof
→ integrated EXP-0025 W1 or one separately controlled successor
```

- `Eliminated hypotheses`: P12 alone lowers the middle-Step proof slope; the reduced run-payload maximum is the binding middle proof owner.
- `Remaining hypotheses`: Separately cached immutable state-hold component or compact current/successor tail authority against the measured P12 baseline; only one mechanism may change in the successor.
- `Stop condition`: Record W1–W4 with exact artifacts and either identify a material P12 gain/new baseline or retain a falsified optimization claim with one named next owner.
