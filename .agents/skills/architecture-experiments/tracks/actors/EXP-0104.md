## Decision Question

On the pinned released binding, which constraint decides the executed per-block ObservationCrossing materialization yield: the configured component counters, the generated branch reservations, the accumulated family-service grant under the rotation, or candidate eligibility? Which single admission/service decision could change that yield while preserving reservation before mutation, fault coverage, accounting already incurred, and owning resource domains?

## Governing Specification

- `Exact section`: `template/pallets/actors/docs/specification.en.md` §7.6 owns the one-third Actor Control ceiling and forbids further throughput work from taking additional guaranteed capacity; `docs/actors-resource-policy.specification.en.md` §2 owns the family minimum/cursor fairness rule and the maximum-reservation to valid-actual settlement boundary; `docs/actors-performance-assurance.specification.en.md` §§1.1, 2.1 owns the two-axis reporting and the reactive reference profile.
- `Invariant`: One scheduler, strict FIFO, Q1, the causal floor, bounded per-block worker work, component-wise admission, one Actor Control domain, and reservation-before-mutation with valid-actual settlement remain unchanged.
- `Reopen trigger`: A measured admission that fits inside a residue smaller than the common worker probe, a settlement exceeding its generated reservation, or a phase yield not reproducible from the measured reservation boundaries.

## Context and Hypothesis

- `Current architecture`: The mandatory prepass rotates the first materialization family, reserves one generated maximum-unit minimum per later family, serves Crossing through the crossing worker in that rotated order, and may re-serve the rotated first family through one bounded lending pass. The worker admits one branch per loop iteration against generated reservations: one single-candidate placed owner, one homogeneous aggregate owner, bounded classification probes, and a reserved consumer-fault owner; the unused reservation settles back inside the same block and Actor Control domain.
- `Reason`: [EXP-0103](./EXP-0103.md) bounded the campaign payoff at eight candidates per block and named the shared materialization grant as the executed owner, but it deliberately did not decide which specific admission constraint stops the worker or what a candidate would have to change to matter.
- `Hypothesis`: The executed yields are decided by reservation geometry — branch owner plus fault reserve plus classification probes against the rotated family grant — and not by the component counters, because the richest rotation phase leaves a residue smaller than one further classification step.
- `Materiality`: A decision-relevant difference in the attributed stop constraint or in the reachable per-block rate; closing the retired `10,000`/`100` horizon is not required.

## Derived Metrics

- `Richest phase`: crossing increment `638,690 = 608,478` aggregate owner `+ 2 × 15,106` classification probes; residue `10,503 < 15,106`, so an unbounded component counter admits no ninth candidate under the executed grant.
- `Rotated-first phase`: increment `625,843 = 1,543 + 3 × 162,782 + 9 × 15,106`; the first service settles `194,537` (`base + 2 probes + placed`, one candidate) and the lending re-service settles `431,306` (`2 placed + 7 probes`, two candidates).
- `Cursor-zero phase`: increment `208,100 = 162,782 + 3 × 15,106`; the block reclaims enough control for one ordinary User service (`252,940` control, `233,776` effect) and no more.
- `Grant shape`: the first-served crossing grant is `197,310`, below the aggregate owner's reservation plus fault (`610,007`); the last-served grant is the entire remaining family budget.

## Result

On the pinned binding the executed rotation yields are decided by reservation geometry against the rotated family grant, not by the component counters. One candidate reserves `180,960` and settles `179,431`; the richest phase settles the `608,478` aggregate owner with two classification probes and leaves `10,503`, below the cheapest further admission step (`15,106`); the rotated-first grant (`197,310`) is below the aggregate owner's reservation alone and reaches its three executed candidates only through three single-owner settlements split across the first service (one) and the lending re-service (two).

## Interpretation

Raising `MaxCrossingTransitionsPerBlock` alone changes nothing in the executed richest phase: the residue cannot fund even the next common probe. The candidate lever set is the per-candidate branch reservation (`162,782` single), the aggregate reservation reach (`608,478` batch), the classification probes charged under a reduced grant (`15,106` each), and the rotated reservation schedule that sizes each grant. Unused reservation settles back inside the block and Actor Control domain and funds ordinary service only when a whole service unit fits, so the reclaim is real but creates no additional materialization capacity inside the same block. Candidate A is rejected as the operative stop because the executed yields persist without reaching the counters' own refusal; candidates B and C jointly own the stop: the grant decides which reservations are reachable, and the reservation decides what fits inside the grant. Candidate D explains only the final zero-yield block after the whole cohort is served.

## Decision

Accepted as a research result for the executed Crossing materialization constraint attribution. The finite stopping basis is that the measured boundary probes and per-phase increments decide the attribution; no further measurement of this claim is required. Decision scope: executed baseline constraint reconciliation only; no physical architecture, semantic, or resource-policy change is selected.

| Candidate | Decision | Evidence-backed reason |

| B Aggregate owner as the only constraint | Rejected | The rotated-first and cursor-zero grants are below the aggregate reservation and still admit one and three candidates through single owners |
| C Accumulated grant as the only constraint | Rejected | The richest phase reaches the aggregate owner; inside the grant the branch reservation still decides the yield |
| D Eligibility as the executed stop | Rejected inside capped phases | Candidates remained after every capped phase; eligibility owns only the final zero-yield block of the drained cohort |
| Raising only the component counter | Not admitted here | A counter or policy change is a separately labelled tradeoff that routes through its owning specification |

## Validity

- `Establishes`: The per-candidate reservation/settlement boundary, the per-phase crossing-family increments and the richest-phase residue, the rotated grant algebra, and the per-phase execution-reuse behavior on the pinned binding.
- `Does not establish`: The exact per-family empty-consumption split (only their matched sum via the empty baseline), the classification path of the very first service on a never-served feed, production-Wasm replay of the boundary probes, or Transfer-service throughput after materialization.
- `Known limitations`: Native full-runtime profile rather than exact-Wasm replay; isolated grant probes do not carry the prepass counter continuity that the executed rotation supplies.
- `Invalidation triggers`: Actor Control ratio, fixed envelope, family limits or minimum quanta, crossing worker caps, rotation policy, generated Weight, or the prepass family algebra.
- `Residual qualitative insight`: The materialization grant is consumed in discrete generated reservations, so a yield step is a reservation-fit event rather than a smooth cost function.
