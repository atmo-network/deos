## Decision Question

Do ceilings of 24 or 16 Steps produce material physical savings over 32 without an unjustified loss of Contract expressiveness?

## Governing Specification

- `Exact section`: `template/pallets/actors/docs/specification.en.md` → Contract bounds and `1.4 Block-Paced Actor Cycles`.
- `Invariant`: Preserve No Ceiling Tax, Q1, semantic identity, Opening/Current behavior, complete lifecycle dispatchability, and identical one-Step behavior.
- `Reopen trigger`: Any proposal to change Step semantics, execution quantum, or downstream policy rather than only the admitted ceiling requires Specification reopening.

## Context and Hypothesis

- `Current architecture`: MaxContractSteps is 32 with inline Step 0 and C6 lazy tail chunks of four Steps.
- `Reason`: A lower product ceiling may reduce maximum Contract, Opening, replacement, close, metadata, and state-hold geometry.
- `Hypothesis`: 24 or 16 materially reduces maximum lifecycle/storage envelopes but does not materially improve one-Step throughput because C6 already avoids ceiling tax.
- `Materiality`: Lower the ceiling only for at least 15% reduction in one binding maximum lifecycle or persistent-state metric, with statistically unchanged one-Step complete Weight.

## Statistical / Variance Notes

Semantic and encoded-size evidence is deterministic. Wall-clock time is diagnostic only. Generated models are reviewed component-wise when benchmark ranges change.

## Result

Both lower ceilings materially reduce maximum geometry and pass the focused runtime Actors suite when the dependent Opening snapshot and predicate-result bounds move in lockstep. C24 reduces maximum create/close RefTime by 18–19% and proof by about 20%; C16 reduces them by 37–41%. Compressed runtime Wasm changes by only 289–291 bytes, and C6 keeps one-Step geometry unchanged.

## Interpretation

The savings apply only to authored maximum geometry. Current C6/tiered authority already removes unused-ceiling tax from ordinary one-Step execution and fanout, while the 32-Step maximum remains component-wise dispatchable inside the one-third Control ceiling. No downstream Contract corpus demonstrates that 24 or 16 is sufficient as a reusable framework ceiling. Maximum-only savings therefore do not justify reducing protocol expressiveness.

## Decision

Retain `MaxContractSteps = 32`. Reject 24 and 16 as materially cheaper maximum profiles but inferior framework contracts without downstream sufficiency evidence. Preserve lockstep `MaxOpeningSnapshotEntries = 64` and `MaxOpeningPredicateResults = 128`.

## Rejected Alternatives and Tradeoffs

| Candidate | Decision | Evidence-backed reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ceiling below 16 | Excluded | Expressiveness loss is large before 16 proves insufficiently efficient |
| Variable per-Actor protocol ceiling | Excluded | Does not shrink runtime maximum geometry and adds policy complexity |

## Validity

- `Establishes`: Comparative physical impact of 32/24/16 under C6.
- `Does not establish`: Universal application-level sufficiency of a ceiling.
- `Known limitations`: Candidate evidence uses the existing generated linear geometry models rather than regenerating identical formulas under each ceiling; runtime candidate builds and focused integration suites validate bound lockstep and dispatch behavior.
- `Invalidation triggers`: Changed chunk size, Contract layout, Q1, metadata representation, or lifecycle owners.
- `Residual qualitative insight`: A ceiling reduction is a storage/lifecycle choice, not presumed throughput optimization.

## Follow-Up / Next Gradient
