## Decision Question

What is the smallest collateral and debt protocol that pays every retained Actor-generation byte, bounds every cleanup quantum before it can be created, preserves mandatory revocation under saturation, and recovers after maintenance interruption without charging future generations for old storage or confusing storage collateral with block computation?

## Governing Specification

- `Exact section`: `BACKLOG.md` N4.3; `template/pallets/actors/docs/specification.en.md` §2.3 and §9; EXP-0129 retained Disabled basis; EXP-0130 sealed cleanup manifest and funded progress.
- `Invariant`: Every admitted generation secures its complete maximum retained storage and terminal cleanup capacity before publication. Retained-byte collateral pays storage occupancy only; generated cleanup authority pays block computation only. Neither may substitute for the other, and no old generation may consume a replacement's hold, slot, or service entitlement.
- `Reopen trigger`: One reachable byte or stale reference is uncollateralized, one retirement can exceed its presecured debt capacity, mandatory revocation can be refused at the hard limit, maintenance can starve under admitted load, or old debt changes replacement/custody semantics.

## Context and Hypothesis

- `Current architecture`: EXP-0130 gives every retired generation a finite manifest and mandatory cleanup cursor but leaves byte pricing, debt counters, hard limits, release points, and interruption recovery numeric owners open.
- `Reason`: Bounded collections do not prevent repeated generations from accumulating to a costly maximum. Charging only active service makes Disabled storage free; charging only bytes does not fund cleanup computation; admitting debt at retirement is too late for mandatory revocation when the ledger is full.
- `Hypothesis`: At generation admission, secure a generated retained-byte hold and one terminal-capacity bond sized for the generation's maximum sealed cleanup manifest. Disabled keeps both unchanged. Retirement converts the bond into classed reclamation debt without allocating new capacity. New mutable admissions preflight a hard global and per-payer high-water bound. A non-borrowable maintenance owner monotonically burns debt units; only prove-empty finalization releases the old hold, generation slot, and residual bond.
- `Materiality`: Freeze ownership, charge/release transitions, accumulation bounds, backpressure, and halted-service recovery. Exact currency coefficients, encoded sizes, service rates, Weight, and runtime implementation remain N5.2 and release evidence.

## Measurements

### Two Independent Resource Ledgers

The protocol keeps two non-interchangeable ledgers:

```text
RetainedHold(actor, generation, payer) = price(generated_max_retained_bytes)
RetirementBond(actor, generation) = capacity(generated_max_cleanup_debt_by_class)
ReclamationDebt[class] = outstanding generated cleanup quanta
```

`RetainedHold` is refundable collateral for consensus storage occupancy. It remains locked while any generation-owned byte, handle, manifest, cursor, or tombstone exists. It is not consumed to execute cleanup and does not claim block capacity. The host's ordinary provider/ED rules remain independent; cleanup never transfers sovereign custody.

`RetirementBond` is admission capacity, not a second byte price. Generation admission reserves enough per-payer and global debt headroom for the complete worst-case manifest that generation can legally seal. On retirement, the reservation converts atomically to exact `ReclamationDebt` class counters; it does not seek a free slot after revocation becomes mandatory. N5.2 decides whether the economic implementation is prepaid credit, protocol allocation, or a typed mixture, but it must preserve complete presecurity and may not silently debit a replacement.

### Retained Object Inventory and Charge Owner

| Retained class | Statuses | Charge owner | Release condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Process identity/generation and live semantic payload | Serving, Disabled | Current generation/payer hold | Replaced by Retired manifest accounting or complete generation deletion |
| Disabled suspended basis, retry/failure/deadline truth and control nonce | Disabled | Same generation/payer hold; disablement creates no free tier | Authorized revival replaces basis atomically, or retirement cleanup deletes it |
| Ring/deadline/Park/Pending node and reverse handle | Serving; stale during detach only | Owning generation hold plus existing carrier slot | Exact detach and generation counter decrement; slot reuse only after local deletion |
| Detector/source registration and stale callback reference | Serving; stale Retired reference during bounded cleanup | Owning generation hold/manifest class | Exact generation-tagged deletion/proof of absence |
| Cleanup manifest, counters, cursor and tombstone | Retired | Old generation/payer hold | Final prove-empty deletion |
| Stable sovereign account and balances | All lifecycle states | Custody/provider owner outside reclamation | Never released or transferred by cleanup |

No post-retirement path may create a new durable old-generation reference: every insertion validates `Serving` and exact current generation in the same transaction. Notifications racing retirement may encounter and detach an already counted reference, but cannot enlarge the sealed manifest. Therefore deadline bursts and stale callbacks change when debt is consumed, not its admitted maximum.

### Charge, Conversion, and Release Transitions

| Transition | Storage collateral | Reclamation capacity/debt | Failure behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Create/reactivate generation | Secure complete generated maximum before first byte/publication | Reserve complete terminal bond before serving | Typed refusal; no partial state |
| Serving → Disabled | Hold remains unchanged; suspended basis is already inside maximum | Bond remains reserved; no debt created | Mandatory revoke still has presecured cursor capacity |
| Disabled → Serving revival | Revalidate current maximum and top up if representation grows | Existing bond remains; fresh capacity only if admitted plan maximum grows | Remain Disabled unchanged on refusal |
| Update/replacement | Secure new generation hold/bond and old retirement conversion together | Convert old bond to exact debt; reserve new bond; check both hard ledgers atomically | Old generation/status unchanged on ordinary refusal |
| Terminal retirement/close | Old hold remains | Convert already reserved bond to exact manifest debt; no fresh high-water allocation | Revocation cannot be refused for ordinary saturation |
| Cleanup unit | Hold remains until protected class is empty | Atomically decrement exactly one class quantum with cursor progress | Roll back object, cursor and debt together |
| Finalization | Release old hold and generation/slot reservation only after bounded prove-empty checks | Release unused residual bond/capacity and delete zero debt/tombstone atomically | Retain all owners if any proof fails |

A reduction in a mutable plan may release only the difference after all objects protected by the old maximum are absent and the same transaction installs the smaller complete bound. It cannot retroactively undercollateralize a retired manifest. Price-schedule changes are governance/resource-policy transitions and cannot confiscate custody or strand already admitted cleanup; their grandfather/top-up rule must be explicit before activation.

### Accumulation Bounds and High-Water Admission

Let `D_c` be outstanding plus reserved debt capacity for cleanup class `c`, `H_c` its generated hard maximum, `r_c` the reserved long-run service rate, and `a_c` the admitted long-run creation rate. Legal state requires:

```text
0 <= D_c <= H_c
admit(new generation/update/revival) only if D_c + worst_case_c <= H_c
sustainable regime: a_c < r_c for every class and for the composed maintenance envelope
```

The hard check is component-wise; spare residence-detach capacity cannot hide exhausted detector cleanup. A generated per-payer generation/debt cap prevents one payer from monopolizing the global ledger. The global cap bounds total old generations, manifests, cursors and stale references. One current Disabled generation is live retained state, not hidden debt, but its terminal bond remains counted. Replacement overlap consumes one current hold/bond plus every unfinished old hold/debt until finalization.

A configurable lower recovery watermark may reduce admission oscillation, but it is not the safety boundary. At any hard high-water, create, update, replacement and optional revival return typed reclamation backpressure before mutation. Pause/disable and mandatory terminal revocation remain possible because their cursor and worst-case debt were reserved at generation admission. No overflow queue, discovered-work list, or generation eviction is permitted.

### Halt, Underfunding, Interruption, and Recovery

If mandatory service receives zero executable block capacity because of an exceptional halt, debt and holds remain durable and non-executable status remains authoritative. No deadline, stale callback, owner wake, or replacement may bypass the cursor. Admission stays closed wherever worst-case capacity does not fit; the system fails closed rather than creating unpriced state.

Under ordinary operation the mandatory maintenance envelope is non-borrowable and independently funded. An underfunded User acceleration attempt changes nothing; protocol service still follows the canonical cursor. A protocol-wide funding failure is observable as retained debt and backpressure, not reclassified as successful cleanup. Recovery restores the same generated phase owners and drains deterministic oldest/admitted cursor order; it does not reset counters, skip stale proofs, or privilege a payer.

Every cleanup unit atomically couples physical deletion/proof, manifest counter decrement, debt decrement and cursor movement. Interruption before commit changes none; interruption after commit cannot replay the quantum. Deadline bursts are bounded by their already reserved class counts. Under restored capacity and `a_c < r_c`, each finite admitted debt prefix eventually finalizes even while ordinary service is saturated.

### Saturation Matrix

| Hazard | Required outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Per-payer hard cap reached | Further mutable generation admission for that payer refuses; other payers remain subject to global/component caps |
| Global class cap reached | Debt-creating mutable admission refuses before writes; mandatory retirement consumes its pre-reserved bond |
| Repeated update before old cleanup | Each overlap consumes independent old hold/debt; the next update refuses before exceeding either generation or class cap |
| Disabled indefinitely | Full retained hold and terminal bond remain; no free cold-storage conversion |
| Maintenance halted across deadline burst | Existing manifest counts bound all references; no new old-generation insertion; admission backpressure persists |
| Cleanup interrupted at final phase | Hold, slot and tombstone remain until prove-empty finalization commits |
| Replacement serving during old cleanup | New hold/bond/namespace remain independent; old decrements cannot release or debit them |
| Payer abandons Actor | Protocol may drain prepaid canonical debt only; no custody seizure or unbounded socialized work |

## Result

Candidate A gives every retained byte and future cleanup unit an owner before executable state exists. Disabled storage remains paid, retirement converts prepaid capacity rather than allocating debt at the terminal edge, and replacement overlap is independently held. Component-wise hard limits bound generations and stale references; mandatory service can recover canonical debt after interruption without borrowing ordinary capacity or touching custody.

## Interpretation

Storage boundedness has two dimensions: a cardinality ceiling and an economic owner. Reclamation adds a third: service-rate debt. One refundable hold cannot prove future block computation, and one maintenance reserve cannot justify free cold bytes. Presecuring both at generation admission is what keeps mandatory revocation possible at the exact moment ordinary admission must close.

## Decision

Accept Candidate A as the sole N4.3 retained-state protocol: secure each generation's maximum retained-byte hold and complete terminal-capacity bond before serving; keep Disabled fully collateralized; convert the bond to exact classed debt at retirement; enforce component-wise payer/global hard high-water admission; prohibit new durable old-generation references; drain one canonical cursor through separately funded non-borrowable maintenance; release old hold/slot/bond only after prove-empty finalization. Runtime realization remains open.

## Rejected Alternatives and Tradeoffs

| Candidate | Decision | Evidence-backed reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Charge only Serving state | Rejected | Disabled becomes free unbounded cold storage |
| Charge bytes only when cleanup discovers them | Rejected | Terminal work can exceed capacity after revocation is mandatory |
| Use storage hold to pay block computation implicitly | Rejected | Refundable occupancy and consumed execution have different owners and failure semantics |
| Admit retirement debt against current free headroom | Rejected | Full ledger can make mandatory revocation impossible |
| Soft watermark without hard component cap | Rejected | Bursts can exceed sustainable and encoded maxima |
| Release incrementally before final prove-empty | Rejected | Lost or stale references become unowned and slot aliasing becomes possible |
| Let replacement inherit old hold/bond | Rejected | Cross-generation cleanup can release or consume current authority |
| Create old references after manifest seal | Rejected | Debt becomes mutable and no longer presecured |
| Idle-only or borrowable maintenance | Rejected | Sustained ordinary demand destroys reclamation liveness |
| Seize Actor custody to cure debt | Rejected | Lifecycle cleanup is custody-neutral and collateral ownership is separate |

The accepted tradeoff is conservative admission-time collateral and terminal capacity, plus backpressure during prolonged maintenance failure. It buys bounded mandatory cessation, honest retained storage, and deterministic recovery without a second cleanup queue or future-generation subsidy.
